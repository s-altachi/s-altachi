# Sheldon Altachi

I’m interested in the engineering behind dependable computing—how software, networks, and hardware interact, how systems behave under pressure, and how technical decisions affect the people who rely on them.

My background brings together computer science, communication, practical ICT support, broadcasting, and entrepreneurship. These experiences shape my approach to technology: investigate the whole problem, explain the reasoning, and build with a clear purpose.

## Technical direction

My long-term focus is systems engineering, particularly distributed systems and AI infrastructure.

I’m developing my understanding of:

- **Distributed computing** — coordination, fault tolerance, scalability, and the behaviour of interconnected services.
- **AI infrastructure** — deployment, automation, observability, and the computing resources behind AI applications.
- **Networking and security** — connectivity, troubleshooting, secure configuration, and resilient operations.
- **Hardware–software interaction** — operating systems, computer architecture, and the relationship between physical resources and software performance.

I’m particularly interested in problems that cross these boundaries, where understanding one component requires understanding the wider system.

## Experience that shapes my work

**Practical technology support**  
My experience includes computer troubleshooting, operating-system installation, device configuration, and helping people navigate digital services. This work has taught me to examine symptoms carefully, question assumptions, and explain solutions clearly.

**Broadcasting and editorial responsibility**  
At Rongo University Radio, I have worked in presenting, newsroom scripting, English–Swahili translation, and editorial coordination. Live broadcasting has strengthened my attention to preparation, teamwork, operational continuity, and decisions made under time pressure.

It also gives me a practical perspective on opportunities for computing in media, including tools that reduce repetitive work and support presenters during demanding live workflows.

**Community ICT literacy**  
Helping people develop computer skills has made accessibility and clear communication central to my thinking. A technical solution succeeds when people can understand it, use it, and work with greater independence.

**Entrepreneurship**  
I founded Synvora Technologies to develop technology services around practical needs. Building this enterprise continues to teach me about ownership, client communication, prioritisation, and the responsibility that accompanies technical decisions.

## How I approach engineering

- Understand the problem and its constraints before choosing tools.
- Consider dependencies, failure modes, and the people affected.
- Prefer clear implementations that can be explained and maintained.
- Verify behaviour and document limitations.
- Treat feedback, investigation, and revision as part of the work.
- Take responsibility for both technical decisions and communication.
